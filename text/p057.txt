CLEAN AND NATURAL PRODUCTS
The  $1.6 billion natural skin care market is hot today.
It’s propelled by cosmetic retailers, social influencers, bloggers, and celebrity endorsers like Gwyneth Paltrow on her Goop website. The pitch for so-called “clean beauty products” typically touts botanical ingredients, with no synthetic preservatives. But are they  as safe and effective as marketers claim?
CarlosGaw / Getty Images E+
ASSESSING BOTANICAL BOASTS
Extracts from plants such as aloe, seaweed, fruits, or herbs
can have a variety of effects on the skin. Aloe vera gel, which is extracted from the fleshy leaves of the aloe vera plant, is commonly used for burns, sunburn, frostbite, psoriasis, and cold sores. Other plant-based ingredients—such as alpha hydroxy acids, soy, and mushroom—might reduce or prevent wrinkles, but so far the evidence comes from small studies and animal research.
Overall, plant-based products are generally safe to use topically, but you should understand that there is little evidence regarding their effectiveness . And keep in mind that they can cause skin reactions in certain people.
RESEARCH GUIDANCE
In the publication
JAMA Dermatology,
an editorial in 2019 gives us some guidance. The article is titled “Natural Does Not Mean Safe — The Dirt on Clean Beauty Products.” Within the piece, two dermatologists at the University of Pennsylvania cautioned that misinformation from non-dermatologists is leading to high rates of contact dermatitis. The culprits are actually the very ingredients that consumers are being encouraged to favor—plant extracts.
Consider also a study of 241 men and women using natural products containing botanical ingredients like aloe, marigold, chamomile, propolis, and arnica. Results showed that 6% reported one or more skin reactions.
What’s more, unsubstantiated criticisms of well-established and safe ingredients are making people fearful and anxious about using products that do not pose a danger. Examples include
petrolatum
(a superior moisturizer that is unlikely to cause skin reactions) and
parabens
(preservatives that are nonallergenic and prevent infections of the cornea from mascara)  .
IMPORTANCE OF DOING YOUR HOMEWORK
So before you automatically reach for a natural product,
research the claims on the label and look for clinical trials to support the use of the individual ingredients it contains. If you decide to buy a product, be on the lookout for any unusual reactions, just as you would for products with synthetic ingredients. Since the FDA doesn’t regulate skin-care products, it’s up to you to be your own best beauty adviser.
Another point to remember: Information about personal care products should come from skin experts, not celebrities. Because everyone has different skin concerns, ask your dermatologist what types of products would be best for your skin specifically.
